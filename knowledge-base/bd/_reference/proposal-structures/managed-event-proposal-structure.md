# Proposal Structure Patterns

## Purpose
Documents the standard structural patterns used in Trescon proposals, derived from analysis of all historical proposals. Used by the Proposal Creator generator as the structural reference.

---

## Pattern A — Proactive Managed Event Proposal

Used when Trescon conceptualises an event and pitches it to a government or institution. This is the most common proposal type.

**Section order (15 sections):**

1. Cover — Event name, "A strategic proposal by Trescon for [Client]", proposed year, Trescon logo
2. Executive Summary — opportunity, concept in 3 sentences, strategic outcome
3. Current Landscape / The Gap — what exists, what's missing, why this event is needed
4. Alignment with National Agenda — how this serves Dubai D33 / Saudi Vision 2030 / India Viksit Bharat / host country strategy
5. Event Concept — name, format, scale, venue, duration
6. Three-Platform or Multi-Format Model (if applicable) — how different formats work together
7. Themes and Tracks — 4–7 content themes
8. Target Audience — segments table (who, why, key titles)
9. Envisioned Ecosystem — types of stakeholders in the room
10. Event Enablers / Activations — formats, competitions, networking, pre-event
11. Expected Outcomes — tangible (MoUs, investment) + strategic (positioning)
12. Three-Year Roadmap — Year 1 foundation → Year 2 scale → Year 3 leadership
13. Activation Timeline — month-by-month from kickoff to delivery
14. Commercial Model / Partnership Structure — funding, revenue sharing, IP ownership, governance
15. Why Trescon — capabilities, credentials, portfolio of relevant past events, next steps

**Tone:** Strategic, outcome-focused, confident. Written for a government decision-maker.

**Visual signals:** Clean, minimal design. One strong concept name. One clean tagline. Data presented in tables or grids, not paragraphs.

## Pattern B — RFQ / Tender Response

Used when responding to a formal government RFQ or tender process. Structured by the client's requirements but follows this baseline.

**Section order:**

1. Cover — "Technical Proposal submitted by Trescon in response to RFQ [number]", client name, date
2. Event Overview — what the event is, as described by the client
3. Why This Event Matters — strategic context, market drivers
4. Event Objectives — map to client's stated objectives from the RFQ
5. Trescon's Understanding of the Brief — show you understand what they want
6. Our Approach / Methodology — how Trescon will deliver (the "how", not just "what")
   - Strategic planning approach
   - Content and speaker methodology
   - Commercial / sponsorship methodology
   - Marketing and outreach approach
   - Operations and logistics methodology
7. Event Format and Design — specific activations, stages, formats
8. Audience and Attendee Strategy — who we will bring and how
9. Timeline — milestone-based from award of contract to event delivery
10. Team — proposed team structure, key personnel
11. Trescon Credentials — why we are qualified, portfolio of relevant events
12. Commercial Proposal / Budget — (often a separate document in tender processes)
13. About Trescon — standard company overview

**Key difference from Pattern A:** The RFQ response must address the client's evaluation criteria explicitly. If the RFQ states criteria (technical capability, team experience, timeline, budget), each must be covered. **The client defines the structure; Trescon adapts.**

## Pattern C — Credential / Capability Deck

Used when introducing Trescon to a new prospect without a specific event concept yet, or when a prospect asks "what has Trescon done?"

**Section order:**

1. Cover — "Connecting Businesses with Opportunities"
2. About Trescon — who we are, what we do, key stats
3. What We Do — the six divisions
4. Our Government Partnership Track Record — key managed events with stats
5. Our Signature Events Portfolio — World AI Show, HODL, DATE, CARE, etc.
6. Case Studies — 3–5 most relevant past events for this prospect
7. Why Trescon — the 5-point capability summary
8. Our Team — key leadership names and roles (brief)
9. Contact — standard block

## Language Patterns That Appear Across All Proposals

**Opening gambits (used in executive summaries):**
- "Dubai's [National Agenda] converges on one mandate: [mission statement]."
- "[Client] is uniquely positioned — [capability 1] as [role], [capability 2] as [role] — to own [the gap]."
- "Most events stop at Visibility. Very few reach Economic Value — pilots, capital, establishment."
- "[City/Country] is at a strategic crossroads in its [transformation area]."
- "No single event dedicated to [topic] in [city]. No prominent [type] event in [period]."

**The gap statement (always present):**
- "THE GAP: [What existing events miss]. [Event Name] fills this gap."
- "[Competitor events] deliver visibility. [Client] can deliver measurable outcomes."

**The outcome statement (always near the end):**
- "Innovation → Capital → Pilots → Establishment. A mandate-led platform strategy — not an events strategy."
- "Not just events — a sequenced platform stack that moves ideas from [stage] to [stage]."
- "[Outcome A] → [Outcome B] → [Outcome C] → [Outcome D]."

**The close / next steps (always the same structure):**
- "Let's make [Event Name] happen."
- Step 1: Finalise and approve → Step 2: Align on scope/budget → Step 3: Announce → Step 4: Launch

## Structural Observations (from Proposal Analysis)

1. **Every proposal has a "The Gap" slide/section** — defining what doesn't exist and positioning the proposed event to fill it. This is non-negotiable.

2. **National agenda alignment is always explicit** — D33 agenda, Vision 2030, Vision 2040, Viksit Bharat, Oman Vision 2040. The event must serve a stated national goal.

3. **Three-year roadmap appears in every multi-year proposal** — Year 1 Foundation → Year 2 Scale → Year 3 Ecosystem Leadership. The target numbers scale by roughly 1.5x each year.

4. **The commercial model is always simple** — client pays costs, revenue is shared or retained based on the model. Trescon never takes the budget risk in managed events.

5. **Credentials are always the last major section before Next Steps** — the proposal builds to why Trescon should be trusted, not led with it.

6. **Next steps are always action-specific, numbered, and short** — 3–4 steps maximum. No vague "let's discuss further."

7. **The cover always has the event name as the hero** — not Trescon's name. Trescon's name appears smaller, as the delivery partner.
