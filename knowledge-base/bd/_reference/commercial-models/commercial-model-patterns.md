# Commercial Model Patterns

## Purpose
Reference file for all commercial models Trescon uses when structuring event partnerships. Used by the Proposal Creator generator and BD teams when building new proposals.

---

## Model 1 — Client-Funded Management (Most Common for Managed Events)

**When used:** Government or enterprise client wants Trescon to manage an event under their brand/patronage. Client has a budget and wants professional execution.

**Structure:**
- Client covers 100% of direct event costs: venue, AV/production, speaker logistics, catering, branding, marketing, hospitality, and Trescon's management and operations fee
- Revenue from sponsorships and exhibition goes to the client (or is shared — see Model 2)
- Trescon delivers: strategy, programming, speaker acquisition, commercial (sponsorship/delegate), marketing, operations, reporting

**In proposals, describe as:**
> "[Client] covers direct event costs including venue, production, hospitality, speaker travel, and Trescon's management and operations fee. Trescon delivers end-to-end conceptualisation, planning, commercialisation, and execution."

**Examples:** Bengaluru Skill Summit, STRIPE Oman (proposed), World Space Economy Summit (proposed), DSO FutureTech (proposed)

---

## Model 2 — Co-Ownership with Revenue Sharing (Partnership Model)

**When used:** Trescon and the client are co-creating an event where both have skin in the game. The client provides institutional authority and infrastructure; Trescon provides execution and commercialisation.

**Structure:**
- Client covers direct costs (venue, production, speaker logistics, Trescon management fee)
- Event revenue generated through sponsorships, exhibition, ticketing, country pavilions, government support
- Net revenue post-expense reconciliation split 50:50 between Trescon and client
- IP co-owned by both parties
- Joint Steering Committee for governance

**In proposals, describe as:**
> "Net revenue post-expense reconciliation will be shared 50:50 between [Client] and Trescon. The event IP will be co-owned by both parties. A Joint Steering Committee with representatives from [Client], Trescon, and other key stakeholders will govern the partnership."

**Examples:** Dubai STEM Summit (proposed with TECOM), STRIPE Oman (with OIA), Sharjah Next Sustainability (with SPARK/Wizcraft)

---

## Model 3 — Trescon-Funded Signature Event (Proprietary Brand)

**When used:** Trescon conceives, funds, and runs its own proprietary event brands. No client — Trescon owns the IP and takes all revenue risk.

**Structure:**
- Trescon funds all costs from own operating budget
- All revenue (sponsorship, exhibition, delegate fees) to Trescon
- Government endorsement / patronage sought but not funded by government
- Trescon owns 100% of IP

**Examples:** World AI Show, HODL, DATE, CARE, World FinTech Show, Big CIO Show, World CX Summit

---

## Model 4 — RFQ / Tender Response (Bid for Contract)

**When used:** A government body or large enterprise issues a formal Request for Quotation (RFQ) or Request for Proposal (RFP) for event management services. Trescon responds competitively.

**Structure:**
- Client defines scope, budget ceiling, and evaluation criteria in the RFQ
- Trescon submits a technical proposal (methodology, team, approach) plus commercial proposal (fee, timeline, deliverables)
- If selected, Trescon is contracted as the Event Management Partner / Production Agency
- IP typically owned by the client

**Key differences from other models:**
- Trescon is a vendor, not a co-owner
- Budget and scope are defined by the client, not negotiated
- Evaluation criteria must be addressed explicitly in the proposal
- Must include a methodology / approach section explaining *how* Trescon will deliver

**Examples:** Dubai Land Department LivingSphere (RFQ-623-2025), Sharjah Next Sustainability (technical proposal)

## Revenue Streams (Standard Menu)

Use this list when building the revenue section of any proposal:

| Revenue Stream | Notes |
|---|---|
| Headline / presenting sponsorship | Largest single revenue item, client-exclusive branding |
| Gold sponsorship | Premium tier, multiple slots |
| Silver sponsorship | Mid-tier, volume play |
| Bronze / supporting sponsorship | Entry-level, many slots |
| Exhibition stands | Per sqm or per stand |
| Country pavilions | Multi-stand packages for national trade bodies |
| Government support / grant | Host government funding |
| Delegate tickets | VIP / Standard / Group rates |
| Workshop / masterclass fees | Premium paid sessions |
| Startup competition entry fees | Small per-company fee |
| Digital / virtual participation | For hybrid formats |
| Media partnerships | Value-in-kind, not cash |

## Investment Recovery Trajectory (Standard 3-Year Model)

For multi-year proposals, use this standard recovery arc:

| Year | Estimated Recovery | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Year 1 | 20–40% | Platform establishment, brand positioning, proof of concept |
| Year 2 | 40–70% | Expanded sponsorship base, industry engagement |
| Year 3 | 70–100% | Financial sustainability through corporate partnerships and institutional memberships |

**Language:** "Positioned as a strategic investment in a long-horizon platform — ROI measured in ecosystem outcomes, not immediate ticket sales."

## Indicative Budget Benchmarks

These are approximate ranges based on past proposals. Use as internal reference only — not for external quoting without project-specific costing.

| Event Scale | Approximate Budget Range |
|---|---|
| 300–500 pax (curated forum) | AED 500K – AED 1.5M |
| 1,000–2,000 pax (mid-scale summit) | AED 1.5M – AED 3M |
| 3,000–5,000 pax (large summit) | AED 3M – AED 7M |
| 5,000–10,000 pax (flagship festival) | AED 7M – AED 15M |

The DSO FutureTech Festival (three-platform model, 5,000+ flagship + two smaller events) was proposed at AED 9M total for Year 1.

## Governance Structure (Standard)

For co-ownership and partnership models, always include:

> "A Joint Steering Committee comprising representatives from [Client], Trescon, and key stakeholders will provide strategic oversight — guiding thematic priorities, reviewing outputs, and validating outcomes. [Client] will chair the Committee and facilitate ministerial and inter-agency participation."
